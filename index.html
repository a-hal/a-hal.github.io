Things I've built

Team and personal projects, McGill and after

Laser-powered rocket

Team project · Nov 2023 – Apr 2024

50 barchamber rating
350 Wcontinuous laser
15,000 Kplasma core
6 monthson time, on budget

A fully functional laser thermal propulsion test apparatus for research. I designed the aluminum ionization chamber, rated for 50 bar with a 350 W continuous laser running for up to 30 seconds.

The hard part was heat. A first-principles heat transfer study sized the heat sinks and regenerative cooling around a 15,000 K plasma core. I made the production-ready DFMA CAD models, GD&T drawings and assemblies in SolidWorks, and led 3D printing, prototyping and high-precision manufacturing of the main components in-house at McGill.

I also managed the budget and procurement of outsourced parts. We delivered on a tight 6-month timeline, within budget, passed structural integrity tests and produced measurable thrust.

Now I'm developing a small transformer-based language model to explore foundational NLP and attention mechanisms. It sits alongside the Machine Learning Specialization from Stanford.

Auxiliary transmission box

Team project · Mar – Apr 2022

A double-reduction spur gear transmission for a small aircraft, to the AGMA standard. We sized a compact gear set to hit the required reduction ratio under strict dimensional constraints, then ran AGMA force, stress and fatigue analysis so the gears would last.

Shafts were engineered with free-body, shear and bending moment diagrams; bearings and keys were selected for fit and load capacity over their lifecycle. Python scripts and custom spreadsheets drove each design iteration.

Structural bracket

Team project · Sep – Dec 2022

A lightweight 2D acrylic bracket designed for maximum strength at a target stiffness. FEA in Abaqus, calibrated with material test data, guided every change.

I optimized a triangular mesh within a 1,000-node limit by focusing on the critical stress areas, and refined the geometry to cut weight while keeping its structural integrity. The final CAD and DXF files from SolidWorks went straight to the laser cutter.
